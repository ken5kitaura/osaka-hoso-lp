Tell

Their story, to the next generation.

A company that has lasted for decades has reasons it lasted. How it treats its customers, what it insists on in its work, the calls it made to get through hard times. But that story usually lives only in the owner and in the customers who have been around the longest.

If young people don't know a company, they don't come to work there. New customers don't find it either. Together with Radio Osaka, we turn that story into words, sound, video, writing and photos, and carry it to the next generation.

  • Words

    The things an owner has said for decades carry the company's whole way of thinking. What they always say at the morning meeting, what they've told every new hire, the phrase they use when a customer asks a favor. An interviewer draws it out slowly, and we keep it in the owner's own words.

  • Sound

    Sound is radio's strength. The tone of the owner's voice, the pauses between words, a laugh that slips out. Machines running, a conversation at the shop counter, a phone ringing in the back office. Sound carries the time a company has lived through, without explaining a thing.

  • Video

    A craftsman's hands, the steps it takes to make a product, the faces of the people on the floor. Video keeps the feel of work that words can't fully explain. The moment someone thinks "I'd like to work here" is usually a moment like this.

  • Writing

    How the company started, the hard years, and how it got through them. We turn the company's history into something people enjoy reading — a web article, an anniversary book, or a book of its own.

  • Photos

    Founding-era photos sleeping in an old album, old signboards and ledgers. Set beside photos of the floor today, they show at a glance the years a company has built up.

All of this can come from a single interview. After it airs, it keeps working — on the recruiting page, in sales materials, at the anniversary event. It stays with the company as an asset it can use again and again.

What we do

What we can make

  • On the airOn a Radio Osaka program, the owner talks about the company's history and where it's headed. Not reading from a script — in conversation with an interviewer, in their own words.
  • For recruitingWe turn the voices of the owner and staff into audio and video for the recruiting page and hiring events. They show what a job listing can't: the people who work there.
  • Further reachPodcasts, video, social media. It doesn't end with the broadcast — it stays in a form people return to.
  • As a bookWe gather the company's history into a single book. For an anniversary, or for handing the company to the next generation.

Why a broadcaster

Why a broadcaster makes it.

Radio Osaka has carried the voices of Osaka since it went on air in 1958. For decades it has listened to people and turned what they say into something others want to hear.

We draw out the owner's story and shape it so listeners think, "That's an interesting company." What we make doesn't vanish after one airing. It stays as an asset of the company.
